The suggestion strip is the row of tappable words above the keys: completions while you're mid-word, corrections when what you typed doesn't look like a real word, and next-word predictions once you've finished one. All three come out of the same on-device engine, which also learns your own vocabulary and phrasing the more you type.

  {/* shot: smart/suggestion-strip-anatomy */}
</PhoneFrame>

This page covers the strip itself: its layout, the completion/correction/prediction engine, and how ranking adapts to you. A few things that also live in the strip have their own pages: [autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/)'s confidence math, the [personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) screen, the [suggestion blacklist](/smart/blacklist/), [smart chips](/smart/chips/) (calculator, currency, unit conversion), [password-manager autofill chips](/smart/autofill/), [contact and app-name suggestions](/smart/contacts/), and [emoji suggestions](/emoji/customization/). Each is linked from the relevant section below rather than repeated here. Everything on this page is about the Latin/English-style strip; a conversion IME like pinyin or kana&rarr;kanji draws a different, scrolling candidate row that isn't covered here.

## What's on the strip

Word candidates fill up to three slots. By default the strongest candidate (the one autocorrect would pick) sits in the center with the runner-up to its left, the layout most keyboards use; turning off **Best suggestion in the middle** (see [Options](#options)) ranks all three left to right instead.

Up to four predicted emoji can ride after the word chips (see [Emoji in the strip](#emoji-in-the-strip)), and a row of punctuation marks can ride after that (see [Punctuation chips](#punctuation-chips)), but never both at once. The emoji tail always wins the shared space; punctuation only appears when no emoji prediction is offered.

<PhoneFrame caption="Word candidates followed by predicted emoji.">
  {/* shot: smart/suggestion-strip-emoji-tail */}
</PhoneFrame>

The strip isn't a fixed area. It's the same row the toolbar uses, and several other features can claim it instead of word candidates: a [smart chip](/smart/chips/) (calculator, currency, unit conversion) takes the full row when it has an answer, [autofill chips](/smart/autofill/) take it over completely on a login field, a "paste your last copy" or one-time-code chip can appear from the [clipboard tool](/tools/clipboard/), and typing `:` turns the row into [inline emoji search](/emoji/customization/#inline-emoji-search-and-shortcodes) instead of word candidates. Any of these counts as "the strip has something to show," so the row rests on suggestions rather than falling back to the toolbar. **Suggestions bar always visible** (see [Options](#options)) keeps the strip as the resting view even before any of them has anything to offer.

## Completions, corrections, and predictions

One engine produces everything in the word-candidate slots, drawing from several sources depending on what you've typed:

- **Completions.** While you're mid-word, every source that has a word starting with what you've typed contributes a candidate: the active dictionary, any downloaded or custom-imported dictionary, a secondary language's dictionary if you type bilingually, your own [personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) of learned words, and (if you've turned them on) [contacts, contact emails, and app names](/smart/contacts/). If the word you've typed isn't a space away from being real but *splitting* it is (for example, "ofthe"), a "of the" candidate is offered too.
- **Corrections.** If what you've typed doesn't match a known word anywhere, the engine also generates every word one edit away (one letter dropped, doubled, swapped, or substituted) and scores each by how plausible that slip is on your active keyboard layout. The best-scoring one appears as an ordinary tappable candidate (not just as a silent autocorrect target), so a genuinely ambiguous typo like "helo" (evenly split between "hello" and "help") still gets you both real words to pick from, even when [autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/) itself stays quiet because neither candidate clearly wins.
- **Predictions.** With nothing composing (right after you finish a word), the strip instead predicts what comes next: your own learned word pairs first, then the next part of a contact's name if the previous word matched one, then a small bundled set of common English pairs to give a fresh install something to offer before you've typed anything of your own.

<PhoneFrame caption="A genuinely ambiguous typo offered as two tappable candidates instead of being auto-corrected.">
  {/* shot: smart/suggestion-strip-ambiguous-correction */}
</PhoneFrame>

Candidates from every source are merged into one ranked list, and the sources don't count equally. Each is weighted well above plain dictionary frequency, so a source that's more clearly *you* (or more clearly what you're looking for) dominates once it has anything to offer:

| Source | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Bundled/downloaded dictionary | baseline |
| Custom-imported dictionary | &times;100 |
| Secondary-language dictionary | &times;40, scaled 0.5&times;&ndash;2&times; by how much you actually mix that language in |
| Your personal dictionary | &times;500 |
| App names | &times;400 |
| Contact names | &times;3,000 |
| Contact emails (2+ character prefix) | flat 4,000 |

Tap a candidate to insert it (see [Options](#options) for the space that follows), and it's cased to match what you typed: a leading capital gives you a capitalized candidate ("Hte" &rarr; "The"), typing in all caps gives you the candidate in all caps ("HTE" &rarr; "THE"), and anything else is inserted as stored.

## Punctuation chips

Turn on **Punctuation suggestions** (off by default) and a short row of marks (<KeyCap>.</KeyCap> <KeyCap>,</KeyCap> <KeyCap>?</KeyCap> <KeyCap>!</KeyCap> <KeyCap>'</KeyCap>) rides after the word candidates, set off by a thin divider. Tapping one commits it exactly as typing that key would, so a full stop or comma is one tap away without switching to the symbols layout.

<PhoneFrame caption="Punctuation chips riding after the word candidates.">
  {/* shot: smart/punctuation-chips */}
</PhoneFrame>

The row only appears while word candidates are already showing, and only when no emoji prediction has claimed the tail instead. The two features share one slot and never draw at the same time.

## Shift re-cases the strip live

Holding <KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap> re-cases every chip in the strip in real time (lowercase &rarr; Title Case &rarr; UPPERCASE) to match what would actually be typed if you tapped it right then, and reverts the moment you let go. It applies to the whole row, not just the top candidate, with one exception: a contact-email suggestion (anything containing `@`) is left alone, since an address isn't meant to change case.

<PhoneFrame caption="Suggestion chips re-cased to Title Case while Shift is held.">
  {/* shot: smart/suggestion-strip-shift-recase */}
</PhoneFrame>

This is a display rule only. It doesn't reject or change which candidate is offered, and it's unrelated to [reverting an autocorrected word](/smart/autocorrect/#undoing-a-correction) with backspace.

## Emoji in the strip

Matching emoji append to the end of the word candidates once you finish typing a whole word, and the same mechanism can offer an emoji on an empty buffer once it's learned that a word you often type is usually followed by one. This is a fuller feature with its own settings and its own page (see [Emoji suggestions while typing](/emoji/customization/#emoji-suggestions-while-typing) for the toggle, the default, and how tapping one behaves). The only thing worth knowing here is that the emoji tail and the [punctuation chips](#punctuation-chips) above share one slot at the end of the strip, and emoji always wins it when both would otherwise apply.

## Learning from what you type

Every word you commit and every pair of words you type back to back feeds a private, on-device vocabulary that both the completion ranking above and next-word prediction draw from. How strongly a word is taught depends on how deliberately you typed it: tapping a suggestion from the strip teaches harder than a word that was merely typed and committed, and a word autocorrect chose *for* you earns no credit at all, only the word pair around it. This grading, along with adding, removing, and clearing what's been learned, is covered in full on the [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) page. This page is only about what that learning does to ranking: a learned word carries a &times;500 weight (see the table [above](#completions-corrections-and-predictions)), so it competes with genuinely common dictionary words in the strip almost immediately rather than needing to be typed dozens of times first.

:::note
If you type in more than one language, the keyboard separately tracks which secondary language you actually reach for and how often, decaying older commits geometrically so the weighting follows your recent habit rather than your whole history. That's the 0.5&times;&ndash;2&times; scaling factor on secondary dictionaries in the table above: a deeper multi-language topic than this page covers, and not tied to word-level personal-dictionary learning.
:::

## Removing an unwanted suggestion

There's no long-press gesture on a suggestion chip itself. Every chip in the strip responds only to a tap, nothing more. Getting rid of a word the keyboard keeps offering means one of two settings screens, depending on what you're trying to do:

- To stop a *learned* word from being offered, delete it from the [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) screen.
- To make sure a word is *never* offered or auto-corrected to, regardless of whether it's ever been learned, add it to the [suggestion blacklist](/smart/blacklist/) instead: that screen requires typing the word into an "Add word" dialog by hand; there's no way to seed it directly from a suggestion you just saw.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Suggestions" />

| Setting | Default |
| --- | --- |
| Suggestions | On |
| Punctuation suggestions | Off |
| Suggestions in every field | On |
| Suggestions bar always visible | Off |
| Best suggestion in the middle | On |
| Space after a suggestion | On |
| Block offensive words | On |
| Suggestion blacklist | Empty |

**Suggestions** is the master switch for the whole strip described on this page: turning it off also stops corrections from surfacing as candidates and stops next-word prediction, though [autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/) itself is a separate toggle and keeps working. **Suggestions in every field** overrides the handful of apps that ask the keyboard to hide suggestions on an ordinary text field; password fields and structured keypads (number, phone, and similar) never show suggestions regardless of this setting. **Space after a suggestion** adds a trailing space when you tap a candidate, the same as finishing a word yourself would.

**Block offensive words**, listed under <SettingsPath path="Typing / Automatic corrections / Block offensive words" /> and only visible while autocorrect is on, uses the same filtering mechanism as the [suggestion blacklist](/smart/blacklist/) to keep a bundled list of profanity and slurs out of the strip, not just out of autocorrect.

A physical keyboard can also pick a strip candidate by number without touching the screen (see [Hardware keyboard](/typing/hardware-keyboard/) for how that's bound).

## Details & edge cases

- **A correction candidate only appears when the typed word isn't already known.** Anything in the active dictionary, a custom or secondary-language dictionary, or your personal dictionary is offered as-is. The edit-distance correction pass never runs against a word the engine already recognizes.
- **A dictionary word and a contact name can collide on capitalization.** A contact's name keeps its own capitalization ("Wasi") independent of how a dictionary word would be cased; if the same word would otherwise appear twice (once lowercase, once capitalized), only the better-scored one is shown, never both.
- **Missing-space splits need a real word on both sides.** "ofthe" only becomes "of the" if both halves are independently known words. An arbitrary split of an unknown word never appears as a candidate.
- **The strip fades rather than snapping.** When the composing buffer or field empties out completely (deleting back to nothing, clearing the field, backing out of a `:` emoji search), the last set of candidates stays visible and fades out over a short animation instead of disappearing instantly.
- **A contact-email suggestion is never added to your personal dictionary**, since an address isn't a word to learn. Tapped inside a field the keyboard recognizes as an email input, it also commits with no trailing space (the address is usually the whole field); tapped from an ordinary text field it gets a trailing space like any other suggestion (see [Contact-aware typing](/smart/contacts/) for that behavior in full).

For the word-typo confidence math and how a correction is applied automatically instead of just offered, see [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/). For the full story on what gets learned, how strongly, and how to review or clear it, see [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/). To keep a specific word out of the strip entirely, see the [suggestion blacklist](/smart/blacklist/).
