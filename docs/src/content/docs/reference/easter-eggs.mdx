WM Keyboard hides a few small surprises. This page spoils all of them, so turn back now if you'd rather stumble into them yourself.

None of them ever touch the text you're typing: every egg is a card, a caption, or a badge that sits *beside* your typing, never inside it. They all run entirely on the device, and none of them phones home about being found.

## Celebrate your keyboard's birthday

On the anniversary of the day you installed the app, the settings home screen grows a **Keyboard birthday** card under the "Your active keyboard" heading, telling you how many years it's been. The first time you open settings that day you also get a toast and a shower of confetti; the card then stays for the rest of the day and is gone by midnight, no dismissing needed.

The date comes from Android's own record of when the app was installed, so a full uninstall and reinstall starts the count over. The confetti respects **Reduce motion** (<SettingsPath path="Accessibility" />): with it on, you get the card and the toast, and nothing moves.

## Find the hidden mini game

Tap the version row in <SettingsPath path="About" /> seven times — the same ritual Android's own developer options use. From the fourth tap a toast counts you down; the seventh opens **Keycap catcher**, a small arcade game. Keycaps rain from the top of the screen, you drag a spacebar-shaped paddle to catch them, and every catch speeds things up a little. Three missed keys ends the round. Your best score is remembered on the device — and nowhere else.

## Read your password generator's mind

Roughly one generation in five hundred, the [password generator](/tools/utilities/#generating-a-password) adds a second caption under its entropy estimate: *"correct horse battery staple" — xkcd 936 says length beats symbols*. It's a citation of the famous comic, not a suggestion — the quoted words are never, ever part of the generated output, and the generator itself is untouched.

## Scan the inevitable QR code

Sooner or later every QR scanner meets *that* link. When the [QR & barcode scanner](/tools/scanner/#scanning-qr-codes-and-barcodes) <Flavor edition="full" /> decodes a code that points at Rick Astley's "Never Gonna Give You Up" — in any of YouTube's usual link shapes — the result card adds a line above the payload: **🎵 Rickroll detected. Never gonna scan you up.** The code still scans, copies, and opens like any other; you just walk in warned.

## Earn typing test achievements

The [typing speed test](/tools/utilities/#testing-your-typing-speed) quietly hands out four badges:

| Badge | How to earn it |
| --- | --- |
| **100 wpm club** | Finish a run at 100 words per minute or faster |
| **Flawless run** | No wrong, extra, or missed characters in a full run (short runs don't count) |
| **Quick brown fox** | Finish a quote that uses every letter of the alphabet |
| **50 runs** | Finish fifty typing tests |

Earned badges appear on the results screen, with a **New** accent the run that unlocks them, and the full list — locked ones included, so you know what to chase — lives in <SettingsPath path="Tools / Typing speed test / Achievements" />. **Clear records** on the same screen wipes the badges along with the bests and history.

:::tip
Badges survive app updates, but like everything else the keyboard stores, they don't survive uninstalling the app.
:::

## Send an SOS

On the [Morse code layout](/languages/notation/#morse-code), key **S**, **O**, **S** as three consecutive letters and the suggestion strip briefly acknowledges: *··· −−− ··· received. Help is not on the way.* It shows once per keyboard session, waits its five seconds, and gets out of the way of the live sequence readout — the letters commit to your field exactly as typed either way.
